## 1. `foods.json` (USDA SR Legacy)

### Source

* Dataset: USDA FoodData Central (FDC) **SR Legacy** (USDA National Nutrient Database for Standard
  Reference, final release SR28 / April 2018 data), official CSV export
  `FoodData_Central_sr_legacy_food_csv_2019-04-02` plus `FoodData_Central_Supporting_Data_csv_2019-04-02`
  (for `nutrient.csv`, `food_category.csv`, `measure_unit.csv`).
* Obtained from the GitHub mirror **https://github.com/tomwhite/ingreedy-data**, commit
  **`846f8c3acce98def8645dd891605c46093e6ae44`** (2020-07-24), under `data/raw/`. The mirror is an
  unmodified copy of the USDA download (record counts in `all_downloaded_table_record_counts.csv`
  match: food 7,793, food_nutrient 644,125, food_portion 14,449).
* Original USDA page: https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets (the "SR Legacy" CSV).
* Fetched on 2026-10-23. SHA-256 of the source CSVs used are pinned in `tools/build_foods.py`
  (`EXPECTED_SHA256`; the build warns if a file differs). The fetched CSVs total about 37 MB.

### Format

```jsonc
{
  "source": "USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy (April 2018)",
  "idType": "fdcId",                       // extra key: ids below are FDC ids, not NDB numbers
  "nutrients": ["kcal","protein","carbs","fat","fiber","sugar","satFat","transFat",
                "cholesterol","sodium","potassium","calcium","iron","vitA","vitC","vitD"],
  "units":     ["kcal","g","g","g","g","g","g","g","mg","mg","mg","mg","mg","µg","mg","µg"],
  "groups": {"01": "Dairy and Egg Products", ...},    // 25 groups actually used
  "foods": [
    [173944, "Bananas, raw", "09", 0,
     [89,1.09,22.8,0.33,2.6,12.2,0.11,0,0,1,358,5,0.26,3,8.7,0],
     [["1 cup, mashed",225],["1 cup, sliced",150],["1 extra small (less than 6\" long)",81],
      ["1 small (6\" to 6-7/8\" long)",101],["1 medium (7\" to 7-7/8\" long)",118],["1 large (8\" to 8-7/8\" long)",136],
      ["1 extra large (9\" or longer)",152],["1 NLEA serving",126],["1 oz",28.35]]],
    ...
  ]
}
```

Food row = `[fdcId, name, groupCode, common, nutrients[16], portions[]]`

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `fdcId` | **FDC id** (`fdc_id` in the CSVs), integer. *Not* the NDB number. (The NDB number, e.g. 09040 for raw bananas, is in `sr_legacy_food.csv` if ever needed.) Do not confuse ids with the example `usda:167512` in a `js/foods-db.js` doc comment: that id is a Pillsbury biscuit; raw bananas are 173944. |
| `name` | USDA description, unchanged (whitespace collapsed). |
| `groupCode` | 2-digit SR food-group code = first two digits of the FDC category code (`0100` -> `"01"`). Names in `groups`. Group `"14"` is "Beverages" and includes the alcoholic drinks (FDC lists "Alcoholic Beverages" `1410` separately, but no SR Legacy food uses it). |
| `common` | `1` for the 359 hand-picked everyday foods in `tools/common_foods.txt`, else `0`. |
| `nutrients` | Values **per 100 g** edible portion, in the order of the top-level `nutrients` array. `null` = USDA has no value (not zero). |
| `portions` | `[label, grams]`: label already contains the amount, grams is the weight of that whole labelled amount. Every food has a `["1 oz", 28.35]` entry (see below); USDA's own portions come first, in USDA order, and the synthetic `1 oz` is appended last when USDA had none. |

Nutrient mapping (FDC nutrient id / SR number / unit):

| key | FDC nutrient | id | SR # | unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| kcal | Energy (KCAL) | 1008 | 208 | kcal |
| protein | Protein | 1003 | 203 | g |
| carbs | Carbohydrate, by difference | 1005 | 205 | g |
| fat | Total lipid (fat) | 1004 | 204 | g |
| fiber | Fiber, total dietary | 1079 | 291 | g |
| sugar | Sugars, total including NLEA | 2000 | 269 | g |
| satFat | Fatty acids, total saturated | 1258 | 606 | g |
| transFat | Fatty acids, total trans | 1257 | 605 | g |
| cholesterol | Cholesterol | 1253 | 601 | mg |
| sodium | Sodium, Na | 1093 | 307 | mg |
| potassium | Potassium, K | 1092 | 306 | mg |
| calcium | Calcium, Ca | 1087 | 301 | mg |
| iron | Iron, Fe | 1089 | 303 | mg |
| vitA | Vitamin A, RAE | 1106 | 320 | µg |
| vitC | Vitamin C, total ascorbic acid | 1162 | 401 | mg |
| vitD | Vitamin D (D2 + D3) | 1114 | 328 | µg |

The build checks id, SR number and unit against `nutrient.csv` and aborts on any mismatch.

Rounding: kcal -> integer. Grams: 1 decimal when >= 10, otherwise 2 decimals. mg/µg: integer when >= 100,
1 decimal when >= 10, otherwise 2 decimals. Integral values are written without `.0`.

Missing values (null counts out of 7,793 foods): kcal/protein/carbs/fat 0; fiber 562; sugar 1,786; satFat 343;
transFat 3,614; cholesterol 399; sodium 84; potassium 277; calcium 85; iron 80; vitA 875; vitC 461; vitD 2,608.
Consumers should treat null as "unknown", not 0.

Portion labels (built from `food_portion.csv`, where every row has measure unit 9999 "undetermined" and the
text is in `modifier`):

* label = `<amount> <modifier>`, e.g. amount 1 + "cup, chopped" -> `1 cup, chopped`; amount 0.5 + "cup" -> `1/2 cup`
  (common decimals shown as fractions: 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/3, 2/3, 1/5, 1/8, 1/6, 1/12; others as decimals).
* `gram_weight` is the weight of the whole amount, so `3 oz` = 85 g, not 85 g per ounce.
* Leading "tablespoon(s)"/"teaspoon(s)" are normalised to `tbsp`/`tsp` (USDA uses both spellings).
* 18 source rows have amount `0` with a positive gram weight (e.g. "0 cup = 142 g"); they are treated as amount 1.
* Rows with missing/zero gram weight are skipped (there are none in this release; all 14,449 source rows have grams > 0). Duplicate labels per food are dropped
  (first in USDA `seq_num` order wins).
* `["1 oz", 28.35]` is appended when the food has no `1 oz` portion (7,073 foods end with it; the rest already had
  a USDA "1 oz" row). A USDA "1 oz" within 1 g of 28.35 is normalised to 28.35.
* 398 foods have no USDA household portion at all, so they only carry the synthetic `1 oz`.
* 282 labels are long (> 70 characters), e.g. `1 chop, excluding refuse (yield from 1 raw chop, with refuse,
  weighing 151 g)`; kept verbatim because they carry real information.

Food order is ascending `fdcId`. Output is deterministic (identical bytes on every rebuild).

### The `common` list

`tools/common_foods.txt` (359 lines of `<fdcId><TAB><description>`, grouped under `# ---` headings) was
curated by hand by searching the SR Legacy descriptions (`tools/find_foods.py`). It prefers the forms people
actually eat (cooked meats and vegetables, brewed coffee, prepared soup, etc.), with the usual raw staples
(fruit, salad vegetables, raw eggs, oils, flour). The build warns if a listed description no longer matches
the source. Coverage by section: eggs 8, milk/cream/plant milks 12, cheese 13, yogurt 5, fats/oils/dressings 11, ice cream 2,
chicken/turkey 16, beef 17, pork 10, sausage/deli/lamb/liver 15, fish/seafood 27, bread/baked goods/crackers 31,
breakfast cereals 11, rice/pasta/grains/flour 11, prepared meals/fast food 19, vegetables 42, fruit 25, juices 3,
legumes/nuts/seeds/peanut butter 30, sugar/sweets/desserts 16, snacks 8, beverages 14, soups/sauces/condiments 13
(total 359).

### Known limits of the source

* SR Legacy is mostly generic foods. Brand-name rows are a minority (by description match: 45 McDonald's,
  11 Taco Bell, 8 Campbell's, 6 Pillsbury, 16 Kraft, 1 General Mills Cheerios cereal, 3 Kellogg's snacks).
* Some foods exist in several variants (with/without salt, different fat trims, USDA "choice"/"select");
  only one is flagged `common`.
* Branded Foods are a different FDC data type and are not in this file.

### Rebuild

```bash
tools/fetch_source.sh .cache/sr_legacy        # blobless clone + checkout of only the needed CSVs at the pinned commit (~5 s)
python3 -I tools/build_foods.py --src .cache/sr_legacy
# -> data/foods.json  (and prints the verification table below; exits non-zero if a check fails)
```

`build_foods.py` options: `--src DIR` (default `.cache/sr_legacy`), `--common FILE`, `--out FILE`, `--no-verify`.
To look up a food id for `common_foods.txt`: `python3 -I tools/find_foods.py --src .cache/sr_legacy '^bananas'`.
The scripts only use the Python standard library. (Run with `-I` so nothing is imported from the data folder.)

## 2. `exercises.json` (MET values)

### Source

**2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities** (Herrmann SD, Willis EA, Ainsworth BE, et al.
*J Sport Health Sci* 2024;13(1):6-12; https://pacompendium.com/adult-compendium/). MET values were transcribed from the
Compendium's category pages on 2026-10-23 with a web-fetch tool; names are shortened for display but every MET value
and activity `code` is the Compendium's. This is *not* a copy of the official spreadsheet: re-check
a value at `https://pacompendium.com/<category>` using the `code`.

Licensing: the Compendium is a published research resource; cite the paper above, and check the terms on
pacompendium.com before redistributing the full table in a commercial product.

### Format

```json
{"source": "...", "exercises": [
  {"id": "walk-3mph", "name": "Walking, 3.0 mph (2.8 to 3.4), level, moderate pace, firm surface",
   "met": 3.8, "cat": "Walking", "code": "17190"}
]}
```

* `id`: stable slug (unique). `name`: display text. `met`: number. `cat`: one of Walking, Running, Cycling,
  Swimming, Gym & Strength, Cardio Machines, Sports, Home & Yard, Dance & Mind-Body, Outdoor, Occupational/Other.
* Extra keys: `code` (Compendium activity code, omitted where none) and `note` (only on the two pickleball rows).
* Counts: Walking 17, Running 14, Cycling 15, Swimming 13, Gym & Strength 20, Cardio Machines 15, Sports 30,
  Home & Yard 27, Dance & Mind-Body 17, Outdoor 17, Occupational/Other 15 (total 200). 199 rows carry a `code`;
  only `pickleball-social` has none.
* Calories: `kcal = MET x weight_kg x hours` (1 MET ~ 1 kcal/kg/h). Some Compendium values are flagged by its
  authors as estimates; that flag is not carried into the JSON.

### Caveats

* **Pickleball is not in the 2024 Adult Compendium.** `pickleball-competitive` (5.3) is from the 2024 *Older
  Adult* Compendium (code 1552560); `pickleball-social` (4.1) is the mean from a published study (Smith et al.
  2018) as cited by topendsports.com. Both rows carry a `note`.
* The 2024 Compendium revised many values relative to the older 2011 edition; only 2024 numbers are used here, so do
  not mix them with 2011-based tables.
* A few Compendium rows are literature-based estimates; use `code` to look them up on pacompendium.com.

### Rebuild

```bash
python3 -I tools/build_exercises.py     # -> data/exercises.json (table lives in the script)
```
